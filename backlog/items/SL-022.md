**Consolidated (2026-11-18):** one slice for the match-event model and everything derived from it. It absorbs SL-051 (unknown attribution and correction-safe scorekeeping), SL-052 (cards and timeline) and SL-055 (statistics and fantasy reconciliation). It is placed before the Workers port so that Phase 3 ports the final model once (owner decision 2026-11-18). The phone/offline scorer workspace stays in SL-050.

**Issue:**
- Match JSON fields (`matches.scorers`, `assists`, `own_goals`) and the `goals` relation overlap, and `players.assists` was a third, stored counter (retired in SL-021, its values kept in `legacy_player_assist_counts`).
- Event edits and derived totals can diverge; the user reports wrong goal and assist statistics.
- Cards and unknown scorer/assist states are not first-class events.
- `goals.player_id` cascaded on player delete (SL-021 changes it to RESTRICT); the event model keeps that protection.

**Required outcome:**
- **Event history:** one canonical event history per match covering goals, own goals, assists, yellow and red cards, minute, and corrections. Each event references player identities (SL-021) and the team entry.
  - Unknown or unassigned scorer and assist are distinct from "no event".
  - A goal can be recorded before its scorer is known and attributed later.
  - A scorer can edit attribution, minute, own-goal flag, card type, or remove an event.
  - Corrections and removals are recorded in the SL-027 history and never double count.
  - Invalid team/player associations are rejected.
- **Score rule:** D-08 is resolved (owner 2026-11-19, `docs/decisions/D-08-score-and-goal-events.md`): events are the source of truth and the score is a database-maintained cache. Goal count and scoreline cannot silently disagree.
- **Derived results:** define and test the rules for goals, own goals, assists, team totals, standings, leaderboards, legends and prediction grading. Repeated grading is idempotent, and a correction triggers a deterministic recalculation of standings and fantasy points (rule R6). All public leaderboards stay season-scoped.
- **Full port, then retire:**
  - Port every reader and writer: `/api/admin/matches/live`, `LiveController`, `MatchTimeline`, standings, `/api/leaderboard`, `/api/legends`, `/api/home/dashboard`, `season_player_stats`, `/api/players/:id`, prediction grading and regrade, the seed, staging checks and pgTAP.
  - Then retire `goals` and the match JSON event columns, behind guards that compare counts and totals before dropping. `players.assists` is already retired by SL-021: reconcile its snapshot `legacy_player_assist_counts` in the same report, then drop the snapshot.
- **Fixtures:** canonical fixtures with known expected standings and player totals.
- **Season stat lines (rule set in SL-021, 2026-11-18):**
  - Goals, assists, own goals, cards and appearances per (season, player) are derived from events through `season_player_stats` and `backend/lib/seasonStats.ts`, keeping their interface.
  - No totals are stored on identities or memberships; cross-season figures are sums of season lines.
  - Replacing their internals with event aggregation removes the registration-season (match JSON) rule.
  - Unknown attribution counts toward the score but toward no player's line.

**Completion checks:**
- Goal, assist, own-goal, card, scoreline, standings and fantasy calculations use the documented canonical rules.
- Edits and corrections are traceable.
- Unknown attribution is distinct from a missing event.
- Changing an attribution updates statistics and fantasy points correctly.
- Repeated grading does not duplicate points.
- No runtime reader of the retired columns or tables remains, and the migration kept every goal and total.

**Usable in its own phase (rule R10):** this item is not complete until an assigned scorer, in the existing live-scoring tab:
- records a goal with an unknown scorer, attributes it later, corrects the minute or own-goal flag, and removes a mistaken event;
- adds, edits and removes cards;

and visitors see the corrected score, timeline and statistics in the existing match and statistics pages. The flow follows the current frontend patterns (no redesign), exposes the real authorized action, handles loading, empty, success and error states, and is verified through the real UI, API and database path (allowed and denied cases) locally and on staging. Phase 9 (SL-097) only redesigns it.
